Start With the Real Question: What Do You Want From a Pool?

Most buyers jump straight to browsing models. A better starting point is to define what the pool is actually for. The answer shapes every decision that follows.

  • Family use. If children will be the primary swimmers, safety features, shallow entries, and durable liners matter more than depth or luxury finishes.
  • Fitness and lap swimming. A longer, narrower pool with a consistent depth is more useful than a wide, shallow one.
  • Relaxation and entertaining. Built-in seating, lighting, and heating become far more important than sheer size.

The Three Main Pool Types — and Who Each One Suits

Above-Ground Pools

These are the most accessible option. They sit on a prepared surface rather than being excavated into the ground, which makes them cheaper and faster to install. They suit families who want a pool quickly, renters, or anyone testing whether pool ownership fits their lifestyle before committing to a permanent structure.

Inground Pools

Inground pools are excavated into the ground and finished with materials such as concrete, vinyl, or fibreglass. They offer the cleanest visual result, the greatest design freedom, and the strongest long-term property impact. They also require professional installation and a larger upfront investment.

Inflatable and Portable Pools

These are temporary, lightweight, and budget-friendly. They are ideal for small gardens, holiday homes, or households that want occasional use without permanent commitment. Setup takes hours rather than weeks, and they can be packed away at the end of the season.

▪ Choosing wisely: Match the pool type to your space, your budget, and how long you plan to stay in your home. A temporary pool in a permanent home rarely satisfies anyone for long, and a permanent pool in a rental is rarely a smart investment.

What Actually Determines the Price

Pool prices vary enormously, and understanding what drives that variation helps you judge whether a listing is genuinely good value or simply cheap for a reason.

  • Size and shape. Larger pools use more material, require more excavation, and cost more to fill and heat.
  • Material quality. Steel, resin, and composite frames differ significantly in durability. Liners vary in thickness, and thicker is generally better.
  • Included equipment. Pumps, filters, ladders, covers, and cleaning kits are sometimes bundled and sometimes sold separately. Always check what is actually in the box.
  • Installation requirements. Ground preparation, electrical work, and fencing can add substantial cost beyond the pool itself.
  • Ongoing running costs. Water, chemicals, electricity, and replacement parts continue for as long as you own the pool.

A cheap pool that requires expensive ground works, constant repairs, or early replacement is rarely the cheaper option overall.

How to Order Safely Online

Ordering a large item online requires a little more care than buying a small accessory. A few checks protect you from problems later.

  1. Verify the seller. Look for a clear business identity, contact details, and a returns policy. A retailer that is hard to reach before a sale will be harder to reach after one.
  2. Read the full listing. Confirm dimensions, included items, warranty length, and delivery conditions. Photos can be misleading if you only skim them.
  3. Check delivery logistics. Large pools arrive on pallets and may require a forklift or several people to unload. Confirm what the delivery service actually provides.
  4. Inspect on arrival. Check packaging for damage before signing. Note any issues on the delivery paperwork and photograph them.
  5. Keep documentation. Retain your invoice, warranty paperwork, and delivery confirmation. These matter if you need to make a claim later.

Installation: Where Most Problems Begin

Most pool complaints trace back to installation rather than the pool itself. Getting this stage right protects your investment.

  • Level ground is non-negotiable. An uneven surface stresses the frame and can damage the liner or cause collapse.
  • Use a protective ground layer. A ground cloth prevents punctures from stones and roots beneath the pool.
  • Follow the manufacturer's sequence. Improvising during assembly can void the warranty.
  • Hire professionals when needed. Inground pools and complex above-ground models are best installed by experienced fitters.
  • Test everything before filling completely. Check the pump, filter, and connections for leaks while the pool is partially filled.

Keeping the Pool in Good Condition

Maintenance is what separates a pool that lasts fifteen years from one that causes problems in three.

  • Balance the water regularly. Test pH, chlorine, and alkalinity to prevent algae and protect surfaces.
  • Clean weekly. Skim the surface, vacuum the floor, and brush the walls to stop debris buildup.
  • Run the filtration system daily. Clean or replace cartridges according to the manufacturer's guidance.
  • Prepare for winter properly. In colder climates, correct winterisation prevents freeze damage to pipes and liners.
  • Inspect seasonally. A quick check at the start of each season catches small issues before they become expensive.

Common Mistakes Buyers Make

These come up again and again, and each one is avoidable with a little foresight.

  • Underestimating ground preparation. This is the single most common cause of early pool failure.
  • Ignoring local regulations. Many areas require fencing, permits, or safety barriers around pools.
  • Choosing on price alone. The cheapest listing rarely includes everything you need.
  • Forgetting running costs. Chemicals, electricity, and water add up month after month.
  • Skipping reviews. Other buyers have already encountered the problems you are about to face.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Can I install an above-ground pool myself? Many models are designed for DIY assembly, but ground preparation must be done correctly. Two or more people make the job considerably easier.
  • How long does delivery take? Smaller inflatable pools may arrive within days, while larger above-ground and inground models can take several weeks depending on stock and location.
  • Do I need permission to install a pool? In many areas, yes — particularly for inground pools or where safety fencing is required. Check with your local authority first.
  • When is the best time to buy? Late summer and early autumn often bring better prices as retailers reduce seasonal stock, though ordering in spring means you can swim immediately.
  • Is the warranty valid for online purchases? Reputable retailers pass on manufacturer warranties, but confirm the terms and keep all documentation.
